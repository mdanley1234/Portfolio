Project ORCA is a field-deployable embedded system built for the Cassar Lab at Duke University to replicate real-time marine light conditions for shipboard research. I designed two custom PCBs in KiCad: a solar/battery-powered ESP32-C6 satellite module and a Raspberry Pi HAT chamber controller, integrating 13-channel spectral sensing (AS7343), GNSS positioning, a 24 V power tree (TPS5430 buck converters, BQ25185 near-MPPT charge management, XC6220 LDOs), and MOSFET-driven PWM for high-amperage LED modulation.

The two modules are joined by a dual-path link: RS-485 over RJ45 carrying differential data and 12 V power for EMI immunity, with 915 MHz LoRa as a wireless option. I wrote the satellite firmware in C++ on ESP-IDF, accumulating spectral samples in RTC memory across deep-sleep cycles to hold draw at roughly 50 mA for unattended outdoor operation.

The chamber application is written in Python: a real-time control loop maps received spectral data to LED PWM, backed by SQLite logging, a Flask dashboard, and an automated validation layer that models expected solar irradiance from GPS position and UTC time to flag physically implausible sensor readings.
